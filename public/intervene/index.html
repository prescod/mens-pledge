How to intervene

The 5 Ds of safe intervention

There is no single right way to step in. What works depends on whether you know the people involved. Choose the option that is safe for you and for her. A small action is still an action.

Which fits your situation?

  • He's someone I knowA friend, teammate, roommate, coworker or family member
  • I don't know themA stranger in a bar, on transit, at an event or on the street

If anyone is in immediate danger, call 911.

Say where you are and what you see. Stay on the line if it is safe to do so.

Your safety matters too.

You never have to fight anyone. Distract, Delegate and Delay all work without a confrontation.

When he's someone you know

Most of the time, the man causing harm isn't a stranger. He's someone's friend, teammate, roommate or brother, and the people who could stop him are the people around him. That makes it harder to speak up, but it also gives you something a stranger doesn't have: he knows you, and he's more likely to listen to you.

You don't need to be certain something is wrong before you act. Speaking up costs a little awkwardness. Staying quiet can cost her far more.

You don't have to be in the room. The pledge covers what you know, not only what you see. If he tells you what he's planning, if you hear it's happening right now, or if he brags about it afterwards, that's your moment too. Before or during, step in with any of the 5 Ds below. Afterwards, don't laugh it off: tell him plainly that it wasn't okay, check on her if you can, and let her decide what happens next.

Earlier is easier. If he keeps pushing drinks on her, won't take no for an answer, or is trying to get her alone, you don't have to wait. Step in now, while it's still easy to do.

  1. 01

    Direct

    Say something. He knows you.

    Pull him aside and say it plainly: "Stop. She's too drunk. Leave her alone." Keep it short and calm. You don't have to win the argument. You only have to stop what's happening. Then make sure she's okay.

  2. 02

    Distract

    Break it up without a scene.

    Give him a reason to leave the room: "Come help me with something," "We're heading out, you're coming," "Let's get food." Or interrupt by walking in, turning on the lights or starting a conversation. The aim is to break the moment and separate them.

  3. 03

    Delegate

    Don't do it alone.

    Get another friend or two on your side. A group saying "we're done here" is hard to ignore. Tell the host, or call someone he listens to. Call 911 if there is danger.

  4. 04

    Delay

    It isn't over when the night ends.

    Talk to him when he's sober. Tell him plainly it wasn't okay, and don't help him explain it away. Don't let the group turn it into a joke or a secret. Check in with her too, and respect what she decides.

  5. 05

    Document

    Record the behaviour, never the assault.

    If someone is already stepping in, you can record what leads up to it: him pushing drinks on her, pressuring her, not taking no for an answer. Never film anyone undressed or being sexually assaulted. Stop it or call 911 instead. Write down the time, the place and who was there while it's fresh. If she decides to report it, a friend's account can matter. Never share anything in a group chat without her consent.

When you don't know them

  1. 01

    Direct

    Speak up, if it is safe.

    Tell the person causing harm to stop, or ask her directly: "Are you okay? Do you want to come with me?" Keep it short and calm. Don't argue, and don't get physical.

  2. 02

    Distract

    Interrupt without confronting.

    Start a conversation with her about anything, ask for directions, spill a drink, or tell her that her friends are looking for her. The aim is to break the moment and give her a way out.

  3. 03

    Delegate

    Get someone else involved.

    Find a bartender, bouncer, security guard, driver, staff member or a group of people nearby, and ask them to help. Call 911 if there is danger.

  4. 04

    Delay

    Check in afterwards.

    If you couldn't act in the moment, you can still help. Ask her how she is and what she needs. Offer to stay with her, help her get somewhere safe, or help her find support.

  5. 05

    Document

    Record the behaviour, never the assault.

    If someone is already helping, keep a safe distance and record what he is doing: harassing her, following her, pushing drinks on her. Never film anyone undressed or being sexually assaulted. Stop it or call 911 instead. Note the time and place, and ask her what she wants done with any recording. Never post or share it without her consent.
